Clay, soft and gloss, before we lock the parts

Clay is the squishy, three-dimensional style: a jelly blob with nub arms, or a hood with animal ears. The new soft and gloss finishes also work on Morph and Gradient, which stay flat by default so 0.2 output does not change. Approve the look, or point at what feels off. Nothing here ships as is.

On light and dark grounds

Finishes

The same seeds in flat, soft and gloss. Soft adds a shadow at the bottom-right and light at the top-left. Gloss adds a sharp highlight and a rim of light. Gradients only, no SVG filters.

Clay bodies

jelly is the blob shape with two nub arms; one in three waves. hood is a rounded hood with cat, bear or bunny ears around a cream face opening.

500 avatars

Builds 500 Clay gloss avatars as SVG strings, then puts them on the page, and times both. Proposed budget: under 100 ms to build all 500 on a mid-range laptop.

12 expressions

Eyes, mouth, blush and brows come as a checked set. Users choose a mood, never single parts, so odd faces cannot happen. Shown on three seeds each.

Shape families

One continuous genome: lobe count, depth, wobble and squash. The family names exist only as labels for the Studio.

Palettes

What to judge

  • Clay reads as soft and squishy at 48px and above, and still as a face at 24px.
  • Nub arms read as arms, not as stray lobes. Hood ears read as cat, bear or bunny.
  • Gloss looks glazed, not wet or plastic. Soft never looks dirty on pastel or mono.
  • Morph and Gradient in soft and gloss still look like the same family as flat.
  • At 24px, every avatar with a face shows two eyes and a mouth, not a smudge.
  • No face looks creepy or sad by accident. Name any seed that does.
  • Faces sit in the body of the shape, never on a thin lobe or touching an edge.
  • The two-dot catch-light reads as one recognizable Opaline eye.
  • Each palette works on both white and near-black.
